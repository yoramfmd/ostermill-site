Finance

Ruth Vaughn

Senior accounts-receivable clerk

Back to our people ↑

Background

Twenty-two years at Ostermill, all of them in accounts receivable. Ruth is the company's institutional judgment about who pays, who is struggling, and which late payer is becoming a problem. She knows accounts by name, not number, and can usually tell you why an invoice is late before anyone pulls a report.

How she works

Four days a week she works the collections queue; the fifth she works accounts: the calls, the histories, the judgment work. Her method is memory plus attention. She reads payment behavior the way a mechanic listens to an engine.

Operations

Dana Okafor

Product manager

Back to our people ↑

Background

The company's first product manager, hired into operations rather than IT, which was deliberate. New enough to ask why; senior enough to be answered. She runs on documents: if a decision matters, she wants it dated, owned, and signed, because she has watched what happens to decisions that live in memory.

How she works

Writes first, meets second. Her previous employer built a triage tool that ran for eighteen months on a roadmap whose second phase was never staffed; she left with a permanent allergy to plans that describe work nobody is doing. Paperwork, for her, is not bureaucracy. It is memory that survives people.

Finance

Marcus Ellery

Controller

Back to our people ↑

Background

The controller, which at Ostermill means the person who signs for the number and answers for it when it is wrong. Skeptical in the productive way: he does not distrust new things, he distrusts unmeasured ones, and he treats the difference as his job description.

How he works

Conditions before results. He wants the threshold written before the experiment runs, because a number agreed after the fact is a negotiation, not a measurement. Slow to enthuse, fast to fund anything that arrives with its own failure condition attached.

FINANCE · COLUMBUS, SECOND FLOOR

Technology

Priya Nair

Architect

Back to our people ↑

Background

Ostermill's architect, which in a 640-person company means she is also its review board, its standards committee, and its institutional memory of why the last integration is shaped the way it is. She carried the 2014 ERP migration and has the scar tissue to prove it.

How she works

She answers questions by building the smallest thing that settles them, and she distrusts any signal nobody is paid to maintain. Boundaries are her love language: she would rather enforce a narrow promise than gesture at a wide one.

Sales

Tom Brindle

VP Sales

Back to our people ↑

Background

Runs sales the way Ostermill has always sold: by relationship, by counter, by showing up. Every customer contact is a relationship to him, which makes him the honest countervailing force in any room that wants to automate one. When he objects, it is usually on behalf of somebody specific.

How he works

Enthusiastic early, protective late. He will champion a new idea in February and defend a customer against it in March, and he sees no contradiction, because there isn't one. His numbers live in his head and his head is usually right, which is both the asset and the problem.
