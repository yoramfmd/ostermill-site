## MEETING RECORD · OSTERMILL INDUSTRIAL SUPPLY

**Subject:** Receivables demonstration (P. Nair)
**When:** Friday, last week of February, 9:40 to 11:05 (re-dated 08-20 per the
authoritative "Part Two at a glance")
**Where:** Columbus, third-floor conference room
**Present:** D. Okafor · R. Vaughn · M. Ellery · P. Nair · T. Brindle · the CFO
**Record:** auto-transcription, cleaned for the file by D. Okafor. Summary first, per
the minutes register.

**Summary.** P. Nair showed a working demonstration, built the previous afternoon, that
drafts overdue-payment reminders from account history. Twelve accounts shown, pasted
in one at a time; five of the readings drew reactions and are preserved. The room
found the drafts convincing. By the end of the meeting the discussion concerned
schedule.

**Decisions.** None taken. No approval, budget, or scope was agreed. The proposed pilot
on the top-forty list was neither approved nor declined. No decision document existed at
the time of this meeting; see the memo of 2 April.

**Actions.**
1. D. Okafor: bring a plan for evaluating the capability on real accounts. Requested by
   the CFO. No date set in the room.

⟨Minute the meeting honestly and the teaching is already in it: ninety minutes, a room
that moved from whether to when, zero decisions, one action. The gap between what the
room believed it decided and what the record shows is the gap Decide exists to close.⟩

---

**NAIR:** Before anyone gets excited, the disclaimers. I built this yesterday between
one and five. It reads the account export, the invoice aging, and the notes field, and
it drafts a reminder. Twelve accounts, pasted in one at a time, chosen by me, no
safeguards, nothing you could ship. It is a demonstration of what the drafting feels like. That is all it is.

⟨A four-hour build is a suitability claim without a suitability record. Part Two,
Decide.⟩

**BRINDLE:** Show the Hartwell one first.

**NAIR:** [reads draft aloud] "...we understand invoice 30291 may have gotten lost in
the shuffle of your busy season. We would appreciate payment at your earliest
convenience, and please call me directly if anything about this invoice looks wrong to
you."

**BRINDLE:** That is nicer than what we send now. I want to be clear about that. What
we send now was written by a lawyer in 2011. This is what I was trying to say in
February. It collects the way a person collects.

⟨Fluency reads as competence. The draft is judged on how it sounds, because how it
sounds is the only thing in the room. Part Two, Prove: semantic against state.⟩

**ELLERY:** Read the notes field it worked from.

**NAIR:** The notes field says "busy season Q1, pays late Feb, always pays."

**ELLERY:** So it wrote a good letter to an account that did not need one. Who told it
this account needed a letter?

**NAIR:** I did. I picked the twelve.

**ELLERY:** That is the job, though. Picking. What does it cost to run?

**NAIR:** Honestly could not tell you yet. I built it yesterday.

**THE CFO:** Marcus, if something like this had been running last quarter, where does
our number land?

**ELLERY:** I cannot answer that from twelve drafts. Directionally, receivables past
terms is eighteen percent in a normal quarter, and the team chases four days a week and
loses ground. If drafting stops being the bottleneck, the number moves. How far, I am
not going to guess in this room.

**THE CFO:** Then the question I care about is when, not whether.

⟨9:40 to 11:00 is how long it takes a working demo to close a question nobody had
finished asking. The bible calls this the demonstration problem: Decide's real work is
reopening it. Part Two, Decide.⟩

**BRINDLE:** We could pilot on my top-forty list within the month. Sales would carry
it.

**OKAFOR:** Ruth, you have not said anything.

**VAUGHN:** The words are fine. The words were never the hard part. [pause] The third
one you read out. Kessler Machine. I would not have sent that one.

**NAIR:** The draft is wrong?

**VAUGHN:** The draft is fine. The account is wrong. Their buyer is out and the son is
running the shop and I hear he is selling. You send a form letter into that and you are
the first creditor in line at the worst moment. That is not in the notes field. That is
just something I know.

**THE CFO:** So we would not send that one. That is one out of twelve.

**VAUGHN:** One out of the twelve you showed. I could not tell you my rate off the
top of my head. Nobody has ever asked me to count.

⟨Three weeks later somebody counts. The number is 41 in one week, and it becomes
Exhibit A. The judgment was running in production for nineteen years before anyone
instrumented it.⟩

**OKAFOR:** Before we talk pilots. What did we just decide this thing is? Because I
have heard it be a letter writer, a collections agent, and a DSO lever in the last
twenty minutes, and those are three different products with three different ways to go
wrong.

**BRINDLE:** It is all three. That is why it is good.

**THE CFO:** Dana, bring me a plan. Whatever the thing is, I want to know when we see a
version of it on real accounts. Priya, thank you. Best use of four hours this quarter.

**OKAFOR:** I will bring a plan.

⟨What she brings, three weeks later, is not a better demo. It is three prototypes built
badly on purpose, run against twenty cases, shown to nobody in this room. And the memo
of 2 April carries a number that kills the project, argued in by the most skeptical
person here, against a room that already liked it. Part Two, Decide, end to end.⟩

**[Meeting ends 11:05]**
