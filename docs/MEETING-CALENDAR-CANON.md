## The clock, stated plainly (added 08-20, Yoram's challenge)

Two years would be long for an agentic build, and this is not one. The PROJECT is
sixteen weeks, demonstration to a rung-2 launch (eleven from the signed memo). That is the speed an
agentic reader expects. Everything after launch is not project time; it is the product's
operating life, and its clocks are not engineering clocks: a quarter of override data
must exist before it can refuse a promotion, a reconstruction request is only a story
because the reminder is eight months old, and drift is time-dependent, not
effort-dependent. The whole arc is about sixteen months. The calendar page carries the bracket
visibly: **idea to production, 16 weeks · governance, ongoing.** (Re-based 08-23 per the book: eleven weeks memo-to-ship, sixteen demo-to-ship; fourteen was neither and is retired.) If canon ever feels
dated on cadence, the fix is a THIRD model swap in the operating year, not a shorter arc.

## The six full transcripts (workstream C, confirmed against SITE-PLAN)

T1 the Friday demo · T2 the adjudication + withdrawal · T3 the go meeting · T4 the hours
debate · T5 the readiness review · T6 the postmortem. These six carry the framework
tags in chrome (the two-column showcase). Everything else gets a meeting summary in the
book's meeting-minutes register, one paragraph, diegetic.

**Summary format, fixed 08-20 (Yoram):** every meeting summary, transcript or not,
carries three fields: **Summary** (one paragraph), **Decisions** (numbered, or "none
taken," stated plainly), **Actions** (numbered, each with an owner, and a date only if
the room set one). Absences are recorded, not padded; T1's near-empty fields are the
demonstration problem minuted, and several Operate meetings will show the inverse, one
decision and no discussion. The fields are diegetic; the chrome may point at what they
reveal.

Note the shape this gives the calendar: five of six transcripts sit in the first half of
year 1. That is not an accident and the calendar page should let it show — the meetings
thin out as the artifacts take over the governing. That IS the book's argument, visible
as white space.
