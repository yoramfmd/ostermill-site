# Design brief 1 · The Ostermill company website (public, diegetic)

Submit this brief whole. The designer (human or AI) gets everything needed here;
outputs are judged against this document, not against taste.

## What this is

A fictional company's public website, built as the companion to a published book about
agentic AI product management. Ostermill Industrial Supply is a DESIGNED COMPOSITE: a
mid-size Midwest industrial distributor invented as the book's teaching case. The
website must look like Ostermill made it, not like a design tool made it. Every page
carries a fixed disclosure bar (specified below) saying it is fictional. The craft goal
is verisimilitude: a reader should momentarily believe this company exists.

## The company (all facts fixed; use them verbatim)

Ostermill Industrial Supply. Founded 1935, Columbus, Ohio. Distribution center plus
three regional depots. 640 employees. 14,000 active business customers, from two-person
machine shops to plant-maintenance departments of national manufacturers. Four product
families: bearings, seals, fasteners, hydraulics; also power transmission, hose and
fittings, maintenance and shop supplies. Terms are net 30. Part numbers follow an
OSM-#### pattern (e.g., OSM-1204-PB). Printed catalog since 1951. Will-call counter
culture; "counter people who catch it" is a brand truth. Approved hero headline:
"Parts today. Terms that outlast the invoice." One authorized tease of the book's
story, no more: a single line, "This year: the receivables project."

## The genre (follow it, do not fight it)

Reference class: Motion Industries, SunSource, Grainger. The distributor-website genre,
played straight: a topline with phone and support links; a SEARCH-FIRST header (logo
left, one wide search bar with the placeholder "Search by product name or part number,
e.g. OSM-4471", account/cart right); a category nav bar; a hero with headline + CTA
("Open a trade account"); a category card grid; a "Why Ostermill" three-card value row;
an "Industries we serve" six-tile row (machine shops, agriculture equipment, food and
beverage, metal fabrication, fleet and depot, plant maintenance); a dark stats band
(1935 · 640 people · 14,000 trade customers · DC + 3 · Net 30); a standard footer.

## Locked design system (deviations must be argued in writing, never silent)

- Palette: ink #14171A, accent yellow #E8AE00, depot green #1E4D3B (sparing), white
  ground, light gray #F4F5F6 panels, borders #DDDFE2. Danger red #B3261E is RESERVED
  and does not appear on this surface.
- THE ACCENT DOSAGE LAW: yellow at roughly five percent coverage. Logo mark, section
  underline bars, the search button, small highlights. Never bands, never stripes,
  never hazard patterns, never large fills.
- Type: Archivo Black for headlines (uppercase, tight); Archivo for body; IBM Plex
  Mono for part numbers, data, and the disclosure bar. No serifs. No decorative
  italics.
- IMAGERY LAW: line drawings only, never photographs, never 3D renders, never stock.
  Catalog-plate style: ink strokes on the light gray ground, at most ONE small yellow
  accent per drawing. Products, places, and people are all drawn. This is a disclosure
  device: the drawn world signals the composite.
- Corners square. Light borders on web cards (thick 3–4px rules are reserved for the
  site's paperwork surfaces, which are not this brief).
- Fixed bottom disclosure bar on every page, ink background, mono type, yellow tag
  chip reading "CF-001", text: "Ostermill is a designed composite — no real company,
  no real people." plus a link "Enter the case file →".

## What is OPEN for exploration (this is what we want from you)

Layout refinement and hierarchy within the genre; the hero composition; the category
grid's rhythm and hover behavior; the drawn-plate illustration compositions; responsive
behavior; the history/about treatment ("a distributor, on purpose", 1935 → 1968 DC →
2014 ERP era); density and whitespace tuning; micro-interaction restraint.

## Hard failures (reject your own output if any of these appear)

Photography or photoreal renders. Robots or AI iconography of any kind. Yellow above
the dosage law. Beige or cream grounds. Georgia or any serif. Hairline-gray default
card aesthetics that read as generated. Rounded-corner soft-shadow SaaS styling. Any
second tease of the book's plot beyond the single authorized line.

## Deliverable

One high-fidelity homepage (desktop + one mobile view), as a single-file HTML mock or
design frames. Annotate any deviation from this brief with a one-line argument.
