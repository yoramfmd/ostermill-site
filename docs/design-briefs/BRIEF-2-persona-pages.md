# Design brief 2 · Persona pages (profiles + the design-phase cards)

Submit this brief whole. Outputs are judged against this document.

## What this is

Two related surfaces for the companion site of a published book about agentic AI
product management. The book's teaching case is Ostermill Industrial Supply, a designed
composite (fictional) Midwest distributor, and its five recurring people. The site
introduces those people two ways, and the difference between the two surfaces is a
content law, not just a style choice:

1. **Profile pages** (one per person, five total): INTRODUCTIONS. Who this person is
   on day one, before the story starts. Bio, role, what they know, how they work.
2. **Design-phase persona cards** (an in-fiction artifact): EVIDENCE. Cards the
   fictional PM produced during the project, rendered as company paperwork.

**THE NO-SPOILER RULE (hard):** profile pages must not reveal what happens in the
story. No incidents, no revocations, no dates from the project. The arc lives only in
dated artifacts elsewhere on the site. A profile says who Ruth is; it never says what
March did to her job.

## The cast (facts fixed; day-one versions for profiles)

- **Ruth Vaughn**, senior accounts-receivable clerk, 22 years at Ostermill. The
  company's institutional judgment about who pays, who is struggling, and which late
  payer is becoming a problem. Knows accounts by name. Works the queue four days a
  week and accounts one.
- **Dana Okafor**, product manager. Writes everything down; runs on documents rather
  than meetings. New enough to ask why, senior enough to be answered.
- **Marcus Ellery**, controller. Owns the number. Skeptical in the productive way:
  wants conditions written before results exist.
- **Priya Nair**, architect. Builds the smallest thing that settles an argument.
  Enforces boundaries; distrusts advisory signals.
- **Tom Brindle**, VP Sales. Every customer contact is a relationship to him. The
  countervailing force, and an honest one.

## Locked design system

- Palette: ink #14171A, accent yellow #E8AE00, depot green #1E4D3B (stamps), danger
  red #B3261E (reserved; not on profiles), white sheet on light gray #F4F5F6 ground.
- These are PAPERWORK surfaces: thick 3px ink borders and rules are the law here
  (unlike the corporate site). Form bars: ink background, white mono type, e.g.
  "PERSONNEL FILE · FORM HR-1" for profiles; "PERSONA RECORD · FORM PD-2" for cards.
- Type: Archivo Black (uppercase names), Archivo body, IBM Plex Mono for labels, meta,
  and stamps. Caveat (handwriting) is licensed ONLY for margin annotations.
- **Portraits: line drawings in one consistent hand.** Ink strokes, minimal features,
  one small yellow accent per portrait (a brooch, a pen, a badge). Never photoreal,
  never generated-photo style. A calibration example exists: Ruth's portrait is a
  simple bust, hair with a bun, round glasses, yellow brooch. Match that hand.
- Empty or unavailable fields render as form stamps (rotated, outlined, mono, depot
  green): "NOT ELICITED", "NONE EXISTS". Never hatched gray boxes.
- Fixed bottom disclosure bar: ink, mono, yellow chip "CF-014", text "Ostermill is a
  designed composite — no real company, no real people."

## Approved reference (match its language)

The design-phase card pair is APPROVED and is the visual benchmark: two cards, FORM
PD-2 form bars, a facts band of four big numbers, a 2×2 grid of goals/frustrations
sections with square yellow bullets, a handwritten margin note by the PM at the foot.
One card is deliberately incomplete (the "affected person": no photo, no quote, VOID
stamps) and its incompleteness is the content. Do not "fix" it.

## What is OPEN for exploration

The PROFILE page template is the real assignment: a layout that reads as a personnel
file or an introduction sheet, distinct from the persona cards but in the same
paperwork family. How a day-one bio, a "how to work with them" note, a quote in their
voice, and the drawn portrait compose on one page. The portrait style's refinement
within the drawn hand. How five profiles feel like a set. Navigation between people.

## Hard failures

Any story spoiler on a profile. Photographic or photoreal faces. MBTI, motivation
bars, or any invented quantification (numbers appear only if someone in the fiction
measured them). Beige, serifs, decorative italics. Rounded SaaS cards. Softening the
affected-person card's emptiness.

## Deliverable

One profile page template rendered as Ruth Vaughn (desktop + mobile), plus a thumbnail
sketch of how the five-person index page presents the set. Single-file HTML or frames.
Annotate deviations with a one-line argument.
